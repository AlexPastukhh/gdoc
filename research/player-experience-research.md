# Research — Player Experience For Game Design

Status: research source
Role: evidence and analytical basis; canonical principles are owned by [`../principles/player-experience-first-principles-and-terminology.md`](../principles/player-experience-first-principles-and-terminology.md)
Edition: normalized repository edition derived from the completed research report; not a byte-for-byte transcription of the original exported document.

## 1. Executive Summary

Player Experience is best treated as a multilayered subjective result of interaction rather than as “the game must be fun.”

Before play, experience exists as expectation and promise. During play, it appears through cognitive, emotional, motivational, sensory and social states. After play, it appears as remembered meaning, willingness to return, discussion and recommendation.

The research supports two strong conclusions:

1. Player Experience is multidimensional.
2. There is no single closed and universally accepted taxonomy covering every valuable game experience.

A stable practical overlap appears across MDA, Fullerton, Schell, PENS/SDT, GEQ, GameFlow, IEQ and PXI:

- competence and mastery;
- autonomy and agency;
- relatedness;
- immersion and presence;
- challenge and tension;
- curiosity and discovery;
- expression;
- meaning;
- continuing motivation.

## 3. Foundational Design Models

### MDA

MDA separates:

```text
Mechanics
  → Dynamics
  → Aesthetics.
```

For the designer, authored mechanics produce dynamics and desired emotional responses. For the player, the experienced aesthetic is encountered first and the underlying mechanics are learned through interaction.

Practical strength:

- causal bridge between system and experience;
- usable experience vocabulary;
- useful reference-analysis structure.

Limitation:

- the aesthetic categories are not a validated complete psychometric taxonomy;
- categories overlap.

MDA aesthetics:

- Sensation;
- Fantasy;
- Narrative;
- Challenge;
- Fellowship;
- Discovery;
- Expression;
- Submission.

### Tracy Fullerton

The playcentric approach begins with player experience goals, prototypes them early, observes play and iterates.

An experience goal is not a feature. It describes an interesting situation or state the player should encounter.

Practical consequence:

```text
experience goal
  → prototype
  → playtest
  → observation
  → revision.
```

### Jesse Schell

The game is not the experience; the game makes an experience possible.

The Lens of Essential Experience asks:

- what experience should the player have;
- what is essential to that experience;
- how can the game capture the essential elements?

The lens model is powerful because one game may require examination through challenge, economy, community, interface, emotion and many other views.

## 4. Empirical Models

### PENS / Self-Determination Theory

PENS connects enjoyment and continued play with:

- autonomy;
- competence;
- relatedness;
- presence/immersion;
- intuitive controls.

It is particularly useful when asking why a person wants to continue.

### GEQ

Common components include:

- immersion;
- flow;
- competence;
- positive affect;
- negative affect;
- tension;
- challenge.

It can support comparison among sessions or builds but overlaps conceptually with other tools.

### GameFlow

GameFlow adapts flow-oriented design criteria:

- concentration;
- challenge;
- skills;
- control;
- clear goals;
- feedback;
- immersion;
- social interaction.

It is useful as a review heuristic but should not be treated as a complete psychological theory.

### IEQ

The Immersive Experience Questionnaire explores dimensions of engagement and immersion. Immersion may coexist with tension or negative affect; valuable experience is not necessarily comfortable.

### PXI

PXI distinguishes immediate functional consequences from higher-level psychosocial consequences.

Functional examples:

- ease of control;
- progress feedback;
- audiovisual appeal;
- clarity of goals and rules;
- challenge.

Psychosocial examples:

- mastery;
- curiosity;
- immersion;
- autonomy;
- meaning.

This makes PXI especially useful for bridging concrete design properties and perceived value.

## 5. Two-Contour Practical Model

```text
Design intent:
  MDA + Fullerton + Schell
  → what experience is intended and why;

Verification:
  PENS + GEQ + PXI + IEQ + GameFlow
  → which observations and measures can address
    the current research question.
```

Do not search for one questionnaire that measures “all Player Experience.”

## 6. Working Taxonomy Of Valuable Experiences

This table is a synthesis rather than a universal canonical taxonomy.

| Experience family | Description | Representative examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory pleasure | Feel, audiovisual impact, rhythm, readable action | Quake, Wii Sports |
| Fantasy and embodiment | Inhabiting a role, world or identity | Final Fantasy, Journey, Minecraft |
| Curiosity and discovery | Exploring uncertainty, finding patterns and closing information gaps | Outer Wilds, The Sims |
| Tension, risk and overcoming | Stakes, pressure, uncertainty and relief | Celeste, Papers, Please |
| Competence and mastery | Skill growth and earned effectiveness | Celeste, Hades |
| Autonomy and agency | Meaningful choice and ownership of a plan | 80 Days, Journey |
| Expression and creativity | Building, personalization and style | Minecraft, The Sims |
| Relatedness and cooperation | Trust, connection and shared achievement | Journey, Sky |
| Competition and status | Rivalry and comparative demonstration of skill | Quake |
| Meaning, empathy and reflection | Narrative, moral or personal significance | Hades, 80 Days, Papers, Please |

Additional families such as comfort, routine and cozy experience deserve further focused research.

## 7. Levels Of Experience

| Level | Example question |
|---|---|
| Moment-to-moment | Is one action satisfying and readable? |
| Decision | Does choosing create agency, tension or responsibility? |
| Session | Does one session have a coherent arc and payoff? |
| Long-term | Does the game support growth, attachment and return? |
| Social | Does interaction create connection, rivalry or belonging? |
| Pre-play expectation | What experience is imagined from the store promise? |

The same experience family may operate at several levels.

## 8. Causal Bridge

A practical extension of MDA:

```text
Mechanics
  → System Dynamics
  → Player Behavior
  → Player Experience
  → Return, memory or recommendation.
```

Example:

```text
limited inventory
  → objects compete for space
  → the player compares, discards and prepares
  → retained objects feel intentionally owned
  → tension and value of discovery.
```

## 9. Experience Hypothesis Examples

### Curiosity

```text
Target experience:
  curiosity and discovery;

Mechanics:
  information gaps, nonlinear clues, controlled uncertainty;

Dynamics:
  each answer creates another question;

Behavior:
  voluntary exploration, hypothesis formation,
  revisiting earlier locations;

Evidence:
  the player creates a new lead without an explicit quest marker.
```

### Mastery

```text
Target experience:
  competence and mastery;

Mechanics:
  readable challenge, consistent controls,
  informative failure, increasing demand;

Dynamics:
  repeated attempts expose learnable structure;

Behavior:
  strategy adjustment and more stable execution;

Evidence:
  improvement that the player can explain or demonstrate.
```

### Relatedness

```text
Target experience:
  connection and care;

Mechanics:
  limited communication, mutual dependence,
  visible helpful action;

Dynamics:
  players infer intent and coordinate;

Behavior:
  waiting, helping, synchronizing, protecting;

Evidence:
  voluntary prosocial behavior and reported connection.
```

## 10. Playtest Evidence

Internal experience is not directly observable.

Combine proportionally:

- player behavior;
- focused interview;
- think-aloud where it does not distort the task;
- appropriate questionnaire items;
- telemetry;
- session outcomes;
- return intent;
- repeated play.

### Observed Behavior Versus Opinion

A player may:

- say the game is easy while repeatedly failing;
- say a feature is important but not use it;
- report confusion only after a choice;
- ask for a solution that would remove the valuable tension.

The designer should diagnose the underlying experience and causal problem rather than obey every requested feature.

## 11. Repository Implication

A Player Experience principle should preserve:

```text
target experience;
causal hypothesis;
observable behavior;
minimum useful playtest;
evidence status;
decision affected.
```

Without this connection, experience language becomes taste vocabulary rather than a planning instrument.

## 12. Research Gaps

High priority:

- reference-analysis anatomy;
- audience, purchase fantasy and store promise;
- short internal vocabulary;
- lightweight playtest question battery;
- cozy/comfort/routine experience;
- links between experience and production cost.

The first two gaps are addressed by the sibling research files in this repository.
