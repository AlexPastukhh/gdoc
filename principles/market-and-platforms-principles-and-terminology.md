This file owns stable planning principles for market legibility, store communication and platform evidence, initially focused on Steam and itch.io.

Platform mechanics change. Official current documentation remains authoritative for operational rules. This file should not be treated as a permanent ranking formula.

## 2. Market Is Part Of Concept Validation

A project should be able to answer early:

```text
who buys this;
what promise is read in a few seconds;
what games frame the comparison;
why this game is chosen;
what playable proof confirms the promise.
```

This does not mean designing the core game for a secret recommendation algorithm. It means making the right game's value legible to the right audience.

## 3. Market Legibility

`Market Legibility` is the ease with which the intended audience can recognize:

- the category;
- the fantasy;
- the activity;
- the expected experience;
- the differentiator;
- the production level;
- the offer.

Legibility is not the same as generic appeal.

## 4. Store Promise

`Store Promise` is the combined claim communicated by:

- capsule or cover;
- name;
- short description;
- tags;
- screenshots;
- trailer;
- demo;
- price and packaging;
- reviews and social context.

The store promise must align with the Player Promise and actual play.

## 5. Proof Layer

A `Proof Layer` is a store or playable element that demonstrates the promised experience rather than merely describing it.

Examples:

- an early gameplay clip;
- a screenshot showing the central activity;
- a demo segment proving the main loop;
- a clear interaction sequence;
- player evidence that the intended fantasy is understood.

A demo is both a product sample and a validation instrument.

## 6. Evidence Classes

Keep platform claims separated:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Official | Explicit current platform documentation |
| Strong empirical | Repeated transparent evidence across relevant cases |
| Practitioner heuristic | Useful repeated observation, still context-dependent |
| Hypothesis / unknown | Closed ranking behavior, rumor or unverified inference |

Do not convert a practitioner heuristic into an official algorithm rule.

## 7. Steam — Stable Planning Principles

Official documentation should be rechecked before operational decisions.

The research basis supports these general distinctions:

- buying and playing are important interest signals;
- languages and tags affect relevance and reach;
- full releases receive baseline New Release Queue presence;
- wishlists are important notification and planning assets but not a universal visibility multiplier;
- followers can receive announcements and events;
- qualifying discounts and demo events may trigger notifications;
- store-page traffic alone is not a direct visibility factor;
- page-to-purchase conversion alone is not the ranking formula;
- store assets should communicate actual gameplay accurately.

### Steam Planning Questions

```text
Are the tags accurate and ordered around the real game?
Can the capsule communicate genre and fantasy quickly?
Does the first trailer show gameplay early?
Do screenshots prove the central activity?
Does the demo confirm the promise?
Are supported languages aligned with target regions?
What traffic source produced the wishlist or purchase?
```

## 8. itch.io — Stable Planning Principles

Official documentation should be rechecked before operational decisions.

General requirements and levers include:

- public visibility;
- a cover image;
- real playable, downloadable or purchasable content;
- accurate metadata and tags;
- language and accessibility metadata;
- devlogs;
- followers;
- collections;
- community circulation;
- honest sales and page behavior.

Avoid:

- tag abuse;
- misleading pages;
- repeated page recreation for a boost;
- fake scarcity;
- perpetual misleading discounts.

## 9. Discovery And Ranking

Neither Steam nor itch.io publishes a complete permanent ranking formula.

Therefore:

```text
optimize for relevant buyers and inspectable evidence;
do not base product strategy on imagined hidden coefficients.
```

Platform algorithms amplify or route existing fit; they do not replace a clear product and promise.

## 10. Wishlists, Followers And Attention

### Wishlists

Useful for:

- notifications;
- demand tracking;
- launch and discount communication;
- selected platform surfaces;
- regional and source analysis.

A raw wishlist count is context-dependent. Velocity, relevance, geography, source and demo response may matter more to a current decision.

### Followers

Useful for recurring communication and community attention.

### Raw Traffic

Traffic is not success by itself. Ask:

- Did relevant people arrive?
- Did they understand the promise?
- Did they take the expected action?
- Did the demo confirm the promise?
- Is the result reproducible?

## 11. Early Positioning Workflow

```text
define audience and comparison set;
state purchase fantasy;
prepare a minimal store promise;
test readability;
test playable proof;
measure relevant response;
revise product or positioning;
scale events and traffic only after the promise is legible.
```

## 12. Platform Metrics As Evidence

Possible Steam evidence:

- wishlist velocity;
- regional composition;
- tag and traffic sources;
- UTM-tracked behavior;
- demo engagement;
- purchase, refund and review context.

Possible itch.io evidence:

- views;
- downloads;
- purchases;
- followers;
- devlog response;
- collections;
- referrers.

Metrics reduce uncertainty; they do not automatically decide design.

## 13. Consequences For Game Planning

The Game Planning Draft should preserve:

- target platform assumptions;
- comparison set;
- store promise hypothesis;
- proof layer;
- important official constraints;
- market unknowns;
- minimum positioning or demo test;
- evidence and next decision.

## 14. Freshness Rule

Before relying on exact platform behavior, recheck official documentation. Record the date or source version for claims that may change.

## 15. Do Not

- Do not treat marketing as a layer added after the game.
- Do not buy traffic for an unreadable promise.
- Do not use a festival as the first moment the concept is tested.
- Do not claim exact ranking weights without official evidence.
- Do not optimize the core game around rumors.
- Do not use misleading assets that promise a different play reality.
