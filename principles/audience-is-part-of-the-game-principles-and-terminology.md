# Audience Is Part Of The Game — Principles And Terminology

Status: active reusable principle-and-terminology owner
Research basis: [`../research/audience-steam-and-itchio-research.md`](../research/audience-steam-and-itchio-research.md)

## 1. Purpose And Authority

This file owns the audience, buyer, promise and comparison concepts used during game planning.

It does not own platform ranking rules, store-operation details or the complete Player Experience taxonomy.

## 2. Audience Is Part Of The Game

The intended audience affects:

- what promise is recognizable;
- which mechanics are readable;
- tolerated friction;
- session expectations;
- desired mastery depth;
- production-value expectations;
- comparison set;
- pricing and platform assumptions;
- interpretation of evidence.

Audience planning is therefore part of game design, not a promotion task attached after production.

## 3. Audience

An `Audience` is not primarily a demographic label. For planning, it is a group of people who already recognize and value a related promise.

Useful dimensions include:

```text
genre literacy;
desired experience;
known comparables;
tolerance for difficulty and friction;
session length;
mastery depth;
social context;
platform and input expectations;
content appetite;
purchase motivation;
reasons to stop.
```

Use demographic information only when it changes a real design, communication or access decision.

## 4. Player, Buyer, Viewer And Recommender

These roles may overlap but are not identical.

- `Player`: interacts with the game.
- `Buyer`: makes or influences the purchase decision.
- `Viewer`: encounters streams, videos or screenshots.
- `Recommender`: communicates the game to others.
- `Creator`: makes community content, guides, mods or broadcasts.

A project need not model every role. Include a distinction only when it affects the current game.

## 5. Audience Sentence

A compact working statement:

```text
People who already value <neighboring promise or genre>,
expect <session/friction/mastery pattern>,
and are looking for <specific experience or fantasy>.
```

Avoid “everyone who likes good games.”

## 6. Purchase Fantasy

`Purchase Fantasy` is the concise imagined role and experience a person should understand before purchase or download.

Examples:

```text
I manage a cozy craft shop and make it beautiful.
I perform ordinary management work inside an unsettling horror situation.
I turn a chaotic process into an efficient automated system.
```

Purchase Fantasy is a working analytical term, not official Steam or itch.io terminology.

It must be:

- recognizable;
- specific enough to guide presentation;
- compatible with actual gameplay;
- meaningful to the intended audience.

## 7. Player Promise

`Player Promise` is the claim about the activity and value the game will deliver.

Purchase Fantasy is the pre-play imagination of that promise. Delivered Player Experience is the post-interaction result.

```text
Purchase Fantasy
  ↔ Player Promise
  ↔ Delivered Player Experience.
```

A major mismatch is a design and positioning problem.

## 8. Comparison Set

A `Comparison Set` is a small operational set of games with explicit reasons for comparison.

Recommended size for early planning: approximately three to five, adjusted when a narrower set is sufficient.

Possible roles:

| Role | What is compared |
|---|---|
| Genre | The recognizable container and conventions |
| Fantasy | The role, world and promise |
| System | The mechanic, loop or economy |
| Production | The polish and trust threshold |
| Price / offer | Expected price and product structure |
| Session | Duration, pacing and return pattern |
| Market | Tags, store language and audience cluster |

Do not maintain one undifferentiated inspiration list.

## 9. Familiar Anchor And Sharp Differentiator

### Familiar Anchor

The recognizable part that lets the audience quickly understand the category and promise.

### Sharp Differentiator

The specific change that gives the project a reason to exist beyond the reference.

A useful early concept often combines:

```text
one readable anchor
  + one meaningful differentiator.
```

This is a heuristic, not a universal formula.

## 10. Audience Adaptation

`Audience Adaptation` changes a design to fit another audience's:

- literacy;
- friction tolerance;
- session availability;
- input context;
- progression expectation;
- price or platform context.

Audience adaptation must preserve or explicitly revise the target experience. Removing all friction may destroy mastery; shortening sessions may break escalation; simplifying information may remove discovery.

## 11. Audience Hypothesis

Until evidence exists, the intended audience is a hypothesis.

Preserve:

```text
audience sentence;
purchase fantasy;
comparison set;
expected genre literacy;
expected session and friction;
why the audience should choose the game;
evidence;
current uncertainty;
decision affected.
```

## 12. Validation

Possible evidence includes:

- interviews with relevant players;
- concept and promise tests;
- store-page behavior;
- demo behavior;
- wishlists or follows with source context;
- tagged traffic;
- playtest comprehension;
- purchase and refund behavior;
- reviews;
- retention and session behavior.

No single metric proves audience fit.

## 13. Consequences For Game Planning

Audience, purchase fantasy, target experience and dominant reference should be developed together. A contradiction among them is an early planning concern.

Example:

```text
reference:
  deep strategic adaptation;

selected audience:
  short low-friction relaxation;

planned design:
  long punishing economy.
```

The contradiction should be resolved before expensive implementation.

## 14. Do Not

- Do not define the audience as everyone.
- Do not use demographics as a substitute for motivation and behavior.
- Do not choose comparables only because the developer likes them.
- Do not promise an experience the current gameplay cannot deliver.
- Do not change audience silently when a reference or system changes.
- Do not treat early positive attention as proof of durable audience fit.
